Every business: 11 card nuggets

What suppliers can charge you, how they get coded, and the settlement that could change who takes your business card.

Grab it →
  1. The code is the store's biggest line, not your purchase. A merchant's code reflects its main business by sales volume, and its processor sets it. Printer ink bought at a hardware store earns like hardware. Visa MDSM
  2. Same roof, different code. When a business inside another one has its own name, its own space and its own register, Visa requires it to carry its own code. The coffee bar inside the supermarket isn't grocery. Visa MDSM
  3. A card-reader app still gets you coded. Payment facilitators have to look at every business they sign up and give it the code that fits. A shop on a phone reader is coded by what it sells, not by the app. Visa MDSM
  4. A surcharge is credit only, and capped. Visa caps credit surcharges at 3% or what the merchant pays to accept the card, whichever is lower, and bans them on debit and prepaid cards. It has to sit on its own line on your receipt. Visa surcharge rules
  5. Mastercard still allows 4%, for now. Mastercard's US cap is 4% or the merchant's cost, whichever is lower. The pending settlement would cut it to 3%. Mastercard surcharge rules Settlement site
  6. A real convenience fee is flat. Visa's rules say a convenience fee is one fixed amount, whatever you pay, for using an extra channel like phone or online. It can't be stacked on a surcharge or added to recurring payments. Visa Rules
  7. Government can charge service fees. Most suppliers can't. Visa allows a separate service fee only for utilities, schools, courts, fines, tax payments and government services. Visa Rules
  8. A $10 card minimum is legal on credit only. Federal law lets a merchant set a minimum of up to $10 for credit cards. Visa's rules allow no minimum on debit. 15 USC 1693o-2 Visa Rules
  9. Your business card has fewer protections than your personal one. Federal card rules mostly exempt business-purpose credit, so the 21-day statement window and the limits on rate hikes don't apply by law. The $50 cap on unauthorized use still does. Reg Z 1026.3
  10. A dollar price can still carry a foreign transaction fee. If your card charges one, it can apply to a purchase from a merchant based abroad, even when you pay in US dollars. Check software and ad bills. Reg Z 1026.4
  11. Your supplier may soon be allowed to turn down business cards. A pending Visa and Mastercard settlement would let merchants decline whole card categories, business cards included. The final approval hearing is set for November 16, 2026. Nothing has changed yet. Settlement site

Every business: 11 tax nuggets

The 2026 numbers that moved, like 1099s, mileage and equipment. Plus the gift and meal rules that didn't.

Grab it →
  1. The 1099 line moved from $600 to $2,000. For payments you make in 2026 by check, ACH or wire, a 1099-NEC is needed at $2,000 or more. It's indexed for inflation after 2026. IRS i1099mec
  2. Paid them by card or app? Not your 1099. The IRS says card and payment-network payments are reported on Form 1099-K by the payment settlement entity, not on your 1099-NEC. Ask your preparer. IRS i1099mec
  3. Payment apps report you at $20,000 and 200 sales. A payment app or marketplace has to send a 1099-K once you top $20,000 and 200 transactions in a year. Card payments are reported from the first dollar, at the gross amount. Some states set lower lines. IRS 1099-K FAQ
  4. The mileage rate changed in July. 72.5 cents a mile from January through June 2026, then 76 cents from July 1. Log the date with the miles. IRS mileage rates
  5. Placed in service beats paid for. Section 179 counts in the year equipment is installed and working, not the year you pay for it. The 2026 limit is $2,560,000. IRS Pub 946
  6. 100% bonus depreciation is back, with no end date. Property acquired after January 19, 2025 can take 100% bonus depreciation under the 2025 tax law. Ask your preparer which fits better, this or Section 179. IRS: 2025 law
  7. Gifts: $25 a person, a year. That's the IRS cap on deducting business gifts. Branded items that cost $4 or less and go out widely don't count toward it. IRS Pub 463
  8. Meals 50%. The ballgame 0%. Client meals are 50% deductible. Tickets, golf and shows are entertainment and get nothing, unless the food is billed separately. IRS Pub 463
  9. Paying the IRS costs more on a business card. The IRS-listed processors charge 1.75% or 1.85% on consumer credit cards and 2.89% or 2.95% on commercial cards, as of September 2026. Payroll deposits can't go on a card at all. IRS Pay by card
  10. Points have no formal IRS rule. There's no IRS publication on rewards from business card spending. The closest text treats a rebate on a purchase as a price cut, not income. Ask your preparer. IRS Pub 525
  11. Home office: $5 a square foot. The IRS simplified method allows $5 per square foot, up to 300 square feet, for space used regularly and only for business. IRS simplified method
