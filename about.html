01 · Meet Leo

Leo didn't start an agency
to study points.

Leo runs Linden Row Insurance in Columbus, Ohio: payroll, clients, a team that counts on him. About $17,500 a month in ads and software runs through his cards, est. He is very good at his job. Points are not his job. So here is how a normal month went, before he had a desk.

  1. Week 1 · The spend

    The big bills went on the wrong card.

    Ads and software, $17,500 a month, sat on cards paying 1 point per dollar. Nobody told him a better card existed for exactly that.

  2. Week 2 · The bonus

    A transfer bonus came and went.

    It opened and closed while he was in renewal meetings. He heard about it a week later, from a friend.

  3. Week 3 · The credit

    A travel credit reset, unused.

    It was on a card he pays for every year. Nobody was watching the date.

  4. Week 4 · The promise

    Tokyo, in business, for two.

    He had promised it. He had points. He had no idea whether they would get him there, or when.

02 · What we believe

Every program has a team.
Owners deserve one in their corner.

Rewards programs are run by smart, full-time people, and their job is to protect the program's margin. Fair enough. But it means an owner like Leo, with payroll, clients and a team to lead, is playing against professionals, in his spare minutes, alone. We think that's the wrong fight. You shouldn't have to become an expert to get what you already earned.

04 · Your guide

Every owner deserves a guide.
Yours has four decades in the air.

Leo is the hero of his story, and you are the hero of yours. Ohad's job is the one a good first officer has: know the route, watch the instruments, and get you there. He has been learning this route since he was a kid.

  1. The early years

    The flight attendant's kid. His father made sure Ohad skipped school as often as he could, to see the world with him.

  2. The software years

    Software manager, fraud prevention. He learned how a well-built system catches what tired people miss.

  3. 2010

    The Peace Corps, Moldova. He quit software for a bumpy bus seat in rural Moldova.

  4. International development

    The Prime Minister's Office, Vanuatu. Nothing went to plan, so he learned to deliver anyway.

  5. UpNonStop

    Award travel, for companies. From Jersey City, helping small and mid-size businesses fly better.

  6. Now

    He built software again, this time to win. No person can watch every program. Well-built software can. His judgment sits in front of it, and your yes sits on top.

Five rules he learned the hard way
  1. Points are milk, not wine.
  2. Flexibility is the real currency.
  3. A transfer bonus is a sale, not a strategy.
  4. The cash price is a decoy.
  5. Loyalty is a one-way street.

Each one cost him something first, like a middle seat in the last row after booking first class, or a trip lost to blackout dates. The desk runs on them so you don't pay the same tuition.
