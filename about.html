Every program has a team.
You have a business to run.

Banks, airlines and hotels each employ people whose whole job is the rules: which spend codes where, when award prices move, when a credit quietly resets. You run payroll, close jobs and answer customers. The rules keep moving anyway.

Stop 04 · the service · 2 min

Four jobs, one thread.
The thread is service.

Earn, Burn, Return and Learn. Each one comes with a pain you already know, a job the desk does about it, what you get back, and the tradeoff nobody mentions.

  1. Earn

    01 · Earn

    Which card for which spend.

    The pain
    Ads, software, fuel and supplies all land on whichever card is in the wallet.
    What the desk does
    Matches each big expense to the card that pays most for it, inside each card's caps.
    What you get
    More points from spend you already make, est. Not one extra purchase.

    The tradeoff nobody mentionsA bonus category only pays if your purchase codes there. The merchant decides how it codes, not you.

  2. Burn

    02 · Burn

    Points into trips.

    The pain
    A balance that sits still while award prices move, and a booking page full of rules.
    What the desk does
    Prices every trip in points and in cash, vets the requirements with you, and books only after your yes.
    What you get
    The trip you wanted, priced both ways, with the math in front of you.

    The tradeoff nobody mentionsSometimes cash is the better buy. And points that sit still tend to buy less over time.

  3. Return

    03 · Return

    Friday, in dollars.

    The pain
    Credits and perks that reset on a calendar you never see, and fees that renew anyway.
    What the desk does
    Watches every credit, perk and renewal date, and sends one number each Friday, checked against your statements.
    What you get
    One number a week: what came back to you, in dollars, est.

    The tradeoff nobody mentionsA credit is only worth something if you would have spent that money anyway.

  4. Learn

    04 · Learn

    Two minutes a week.

    The pain
    Rules change all the time, and every forum argues about what they mean.
    What the desk does
    One short lesson a week, drawn from your own file, in owner words.
    What you get
    You understand every move before you say yes to it.

    The tradeoff nobody mentionsWhat worked last year may cost you this year. Old advice ages fast.

Stop 05 · turn the dial · 1 min

Points or cash?
Ask the dial.

Every redemption has a price per point. Work it out once and you will never wonder again. Pick an example, or type in a trip you are pricing.

Try an example

( cash price − taxes ) ÷ points = cents per point

1.6¢a point, est.

A strong use, est. Better than a plain cash out.

  • Under 1¢: a plain cash out usually beats it
  • 1¢ to 1.6¢: a fair use
  • Over 1.6¢: a strong use, est.

Example prices are illustrative. A rule of thumb, not advice: the right use also depends on your plans, how flexible the cash fare is, and what else the points could do.
