02 How the game is built
How the game is built

The house wins twice. Nobody tells you when.

This is not a conspiracy. It is a business model, published in the annual reports, and it runs on you being busy.

Banks buy miles at 1.25 to 2 cents; typical redemption value runs 1.1 to 1.5. The gap is the business model. Chapter 04 is the way out.

01

Miles are a product. You are not the customer.

$7.4Bcollected by one airline from its card partner in 2024, larger than its entire operating profit that year.

Airlines do not give miles away, they sell them in bulk to banks. Delta collected roughly $7.4 billion from its card partner in 2024, larger than the airline's entire operating profit that year. American and United run the same play. When the planes were grounded they mortgaged the loyalty programs alongside everything else: $9 billion raised against SkyMiles and $6.8 billion against MileagePlus in 2020, then $10 billion against AAdvantage in March 2021.

Delta figure from its 2024 Form 10-K. Loyalty financings from the 2020 and 2021 offering documents.
02

They win on the spread.

Programs sell miles to the banks for somewhere between 1.25 and 2 cents each, and independent valuations put typical redemption value at 1.1 to 1.5. Neither number appears in any filing, because co-brand pricing is confidential, but the direction of the gap is the business model and it repeats across every mile issued. In the 2019 figures the airlines showed bond investors, AAdvantage ran a 49 percent EBITDA margin and MileagePlus 34. The airlines themselves earned operating margins of 4.8 to 9.7 percent in 2024.

Margins from the 2019 AAdvantage and MileagePlus investor presentations.
03

They win again when you forget.

30 trillion
miles estimated sitting unredeemed in 2018. Breakage runs a quarter to a third of everything issued.

Points earned and never used have a name on the balance sheet: breakage. An industry study estimated in 2018 that more than 30 trillion frequent flyer miles were sitting unredeemed, and it is still the most-cited figure in the industry. Breakage typically runs a quarter to a third of everything issued, around 25 percent in retail programs and 15 to 30 in airline ones, and every one of those forgotten points is margin nobody has to deliver. Your balance is in that number.

Breakage and unredeemed miles from a 2018 industry study. See the Ghost Economy on The Daily Redemption for the full working.
04

One move beats the house.

Redeem above the spread. Move the points into the airline or hotel program itself, at the right moment, against the right chart, on the right dates, and the value lands on your side of the table instead of theirs. That takes somebody watching all day, every day. That is the entire job you are hiring.

2 departments are working on your points. Until now, neither one worked for you.

VS

Cash back is not income. That is not a compliment.

💵

Cash backTreated as a rebate

  • Not income, which sounds generous
  • A rebate is a discount on money you already spent
  • So the business expense you deduct shrinks by the same amount
✈️

PointsTreated differently

  • In Announcement 2002-18 the IRS said it will not assert that a taxpayer understated tax by reason of receiving or personally using miles attributable to business travel
  • The same announcement excludes miles converted to cash and miles paid as compensation, and it has not been revisited since
  • Spend points on business travel and you give up that deduction, so it is not free either

One arrives with a tax drag. The other arrives as a trip.

We are not your accountant and we will not pretend to be one. Ask yours which of the two they would rather see on your return. It is a 2 minute question with a 4 figure answer.
