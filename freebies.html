Every business: 9 card nuggets

What suppliers can charge you, how they get coded, and the settlement that could change who takes your business card.

Grab it →
  1. The code is the store's biggest line, not your purchase. A merchant's code reflects its main business by sales volume, and its processor sets it. Printer ink bought at a hardware store earns like hardware.
  2. Same roof, different code. When a business inside another one has its own name, its own space and its own register, Visa requires it to carry its own code. The coffee bar inside the supermarket isn't grocery.
  3. A card-reader app still gets you coded. Payment facilitators have to look at every business they sign up and give it the code that fits. A shop on a phone reader is coded by what it sells, not by the app.
  4. A surcharge is credit only, and capped. Visa caps credit surcharges at 3% or what the merchant pays to accept the card, whichever is lower, and bans them on debit and prepaid cards. It has to sit on its own line on your receipt.
  5. Mastercard still allows 4%, for now. Mastercard's US cap is 4% or the merchant's cost, whichever is lower. The pending settlement would cut it to 3%.
  6. A real convenience fee is flat. Visa's rules say a convenience fee is one fixed amount, whatever you pay, for using an extra channel like phone or online. It can't be stacked on a surcharge or added to recurring payments.
  7. Government can charge service fees. Most suppliers can't. Visa allows a separate service fee only for utilities, schools, courts, fines, tax payments and government services.
  8. A dollar price can still carry a foreign transaction fee. If your card charges one, it can apply to a purchase from a merchant based abroad, even when you pay in US dollars. Check software and ad bills.
  9. Your supplier may soon be allowed to turn down business cards. A pending Visa and Mastercard settlement would let merchants decline whole card categories, business cards included. The final approval hearing is set for November 16, 2026. Nothing has changed yet.

General information, not tax, legal or financial advice. Merchant codes are set by each merchant's processor, so your statement is the final word.

Gut check1 Example1 min

Card strategy gut checks

Five questions the desk asks every new client. Answer them honestly.

Grab it →
  1. If your biggest supplier stopped taking your card tomorrow, what would it cost you? A pending settlement could let merchants turn down business cards.
  2. Do you know the code your top five suppliers run under, or are you guessing? Rewards follow the code, not the item.
  3. Are you paying a 3% surcharge to earn 2%? Example: at 2 points a dollar worth a cent each, a 3% surcharge loses you 1% on every swipe. Example
  4. When did you last read your card's rewards terms? Categories and caps change. Your plan should too.
  5. If an issuer closed your card today, how many points would you lose? Points that sit unredeemed are a balance someone else controls.

Day-one gut checks

Three questions that separate a business from an expensive hobby.

Grab it →
  1. Could your accountant tell business from personal spending using your statements alone? If not, that's the first fix.

Travel smarts1 min

Travel codes, decoded

One trip can touch five codes. Here's how the airline, the hotel and the car show up.

Grab it →
  1. One trip, five codes. Flights 4511, hotels 7011, cabs and rideshare 4121, rail 4112, rental cars 7512.
  2. Big airlines carry their own code. Visa gives major airlines their own codes, from 3000 to 3350, and requires them on tickets, bag fees, upgrades and onboard buys.
  3. If the travel agency charges you, it's 4722. Travel agencies and tour operators code as 4722, not as the airline or hotel.
  4. A short-term rental can be a hotel, to Visa. Visa's lodging code, 7011, includes short-term rentals of under 30 days.
  5. Car sharing counts as car rental. Visa includes car-sharing schemes in 7512, Automobile Rental Agency.

Fees, IDs, borders and refunds. What changed for business travelers.

Decoder1 min

Decoder: card words

Ten words from the back of a processor statement, in plain English.

Grab it →
  • Merchant category code (MCC) The four-digit code a merchant's processor assigns, describing its main business. Rewards categories key off it.
  • Issuer The bank behind your card. It sets your rewards, rate and limit.
  • Acquirer The merchant's bank or processor. It assigns the merchant code.
  • Card network Visa, Mastercard and the like. They write the rules issuers and acquirers follow.
  • Interchange The fee a merchant's bank pays your card's bank on each purchase, at rates the card network sets.
  • Merchant discount rate What a merchant pays in total to accept a card, interchange included.
  • Surcharge An extra charge for paying by credit card. Capped and credit-only under Visa's rules.
  • Convenience fee A flat fee for paying through an extra channel like phone or online.
  • Chargeback A disputed charge pulled back from the merchant through the card network.
  • Payment facilitator A company that signs up small merchants under its own processing account and must code each one.

Plain-English definitions, not legal ones.

Decoder1 min

Decoder: payment rails

Eight ways money moves, and what each one means for you.

Grab it →
  • ACH The batch network behind direct deposit and most bill pay, governed by Nacha.
  • Same Day ACH ACH that settles the same business day, up to $1 million per payment.
  • Wire transfer A bank-to-bank transfer that's fast and hard to reverse.
  • RTP The Clearing House's instant payment network, up to $10 million per payment.
  • FedNow The Federal Reserve's instant payment service, up to $10 million per payment if your bank allows.
  • Positive pay A bank service that only clears checks or debits you've pre-approved.
  • Originator The business that starts an ACH payment, like running payroll.
  • Level 3 data Line-item detail sent with a commercial card payment, which can lower the merchant's cost.

Plain-English definitions, not legal ones.

Decoder: points and travel words

Ten words that come up the moment you try to spend points.

Grab it →
  • Award chart A fixed table of how many points or miles an award costs.
