Every business: 9 card nuggets

What suppliers can charge you, how they get coded, and the settlement that could change who takes your business card.

Grab it →
  1. The code is the store's biggest line, not your purchase. A merchant's code reflects its main business by sales volume, and its processor sets it. Printer ink bought at a hardware store earns like hardware.
  2. Same roof, different code. When a business inside another one has its own name, its own space and its own register, Visa requires it to carry its own code. The coffee bar inside the supermarket typically isn't grocery.
  3. A card-reader app still gets you coded. Payment facilitators have to look at every business they sign up and give it the code that fits. A shop on a phone reader is coded by what it sells, not by the app.
  4. A surcharge is credit only, and capped. Visa caps credit surcharges at 3% or what the merchant pays to accept the card, whichever is lower, and bans them on debit and prepaid cards. It has to sit on its own line on your receipt.
  5. Mastercard still allows 4%, for now. Mastercard's US cap is 4% or the merchant's cost, whichever is lower. The pending settlement would cut it to 3%.
  6. A real convenience fee is flat. Visa's rules say a convenience fee is one fixed amount, whatever you pay, for using an extra channel like phone or online. It can't be stacked on a surcharge or added to recurring payments.
  7. Government can charge service fees. Most suppliers can't. Visa allows a separate service fee only for utilities, schools, courts, fines, tax payments and government services.
  8. A dollar price can still carry a foreign transaction fee. If your card charges one, it can apply to a purchase from a merchant based abroad, even when you pay in US dollars. Worth knowing for software and ad bills.
  9. Your supplier may soon be allowed to turn down business cards. A pending Visa and Mastercard settlement would let merchants decline whole card categories, business cards included. As of Mastercard's Oct 29, 2026 filing, the final approval hearing is scheduled for November 2026, and nothing has changed yet.

Surcharges, debit costs and the card settlement, as of November 2026.

Grab it →

  2. On dual-message networks in 2024, small-bank debit carried nearly triple big-bank interchange. Per Fed data for 2024, on dual-message networks, interchange on debit from issuers exempt from the fee cap averaged $0.61 a transaction, or 1.41%, against $0.22, or 0.45%, from covered issuers.   3. Advertised hotel prices must include mandatory fees, business bookings too.   4. Single-message debit interchange averaged 25 cents a transaction in 2024. Per Fed data for 2024, debit interchange averaged $0.25 a transaction, or 0.57%, on single-message networks, against $0.37, or 0.79%, on dual-message networks.
  5. Debit issuers have to enable two unaffiliated networks, online included. Since Jul 1, 2023, Regulation II has had issuers enable at least two unaffiliated networks for card-not-present debit, such as online purchases. Issuers and networks can't inhibit a merchant's ability to direct the routing.
  6. Massachusetts law bars sellers from adding credit card surcharges. As of November 2026, Massachusetts General Laws chapter 140D, section 28A, says no seller in any sales transaction 'may impose a surcharge' on a cardholder who elects to use a credit card. As of November 2026, Visa's merchant FAQ has a surcharging merchant alert customers in three places: the point of entry, the point of sale and every receipt. Mastercard's surcharge rules call for the surcharge's dollar amount on the receipt.
  8. Surcharging starts with 30 days' notice to the acquirer. As of November 2026, Visa says merchants notify their acquirer 30 days before they begin surcharging. Mastercard asks for notice to both Mastercard and the acquirer no less than 30 days ahead. Worth knowing before adding a surcharge to your own invoices.
  9. Estimated tax by card: two payments per quarter. Per the IRS frequency limit table as of November 2026, card payments toward Form 1040-ES estimated tax are limited to 2 per quarter. IRS.gov notes that frequency limits apply to card and cash payments.
  10. Visa mystery-shops surcharges every year. Per Visa's US surcharge Q&A (Feb 15, 2024), outside auditors mystery-shop each year, on top of numerous consumer complaints. Visa adds that an acquirer may be assessed an immediate $1,000 fine for any merchant identified as surcharging improperly.
